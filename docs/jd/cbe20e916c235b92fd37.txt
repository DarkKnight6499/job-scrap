Risk Engineering owns the systems that execute Imprint's risk decisions in real time: transaction authorization and fraud scoring on every card swipe, the underwriting pipeline that evaluates every application, and the vendor integrations that power both. These are latency-sensitive, high-stakes distributed systems with a sprawling surface area and many stakeholders competing for the team's capacity. AI has made execution faster; the differentiator is the judgment to decide what to build, how to sequence it, and when to say no. That judgment, applied to a strong team and a hard domain, is what this Senior Engineering Manager owns.




THE OPPORTUNITY

 - Own real-time authorization and fraud decisioning: Every card swipe runs through systems your team builds. Decisions happen in milliseconds, failures are visible immediately, and a wrong answer costs real money in either direction: fraud losses on one side, declined good customers on the other. You set the bar for how these systems are designed, tested, and operated.

 - Own the underwriting pipeline: Every application Imprint receives is evaluated by the pipeline your team owns, spanning credit, fraud, identity, and compliance checks. As Imprint launches new brand partners and new products, the pipeline has to absorb new policies and new data sources without becoming brittle.

 - Make vendor decisions that hold up in production: Fraud scoring, identity verification, and bureau data all run through third-party integrations. You'll evaluate vendor capabilities, weigh build-versus-buy with real latency and cost numbers, and push back on approaches that won't survive production load.

 - Prioritize across a wide surface cross-functional surface area: Credit, Fraud, Product, and Compliance all have legitimate asks and compete for the same engineering capacity. You'll bring clarity to poorly-scoped platform problems, decide what the team does and does not take on, and make the reasoning legible to stakeholders who didn't get what they asked for. You define the roadmap and scope the work yourself; nothing is abstracted through organizational layers.

 - Lead with technical credibility, amplified by AI: AI has collapsed the distance between management and IC work. Managers at Imprint use AI tools to investigate codebases, assess the complexity and viability of work, and bring clarity to ambiguous asks. You don't need to be on the critical path writing code (though some managers are), but you need the technical judgment to evaluate design trade-offs on latency-sensitive distributed systems and the modern tools to exercise it.

 - Lead a strong team as a craft: You inherit a strong, psychologically safe team of senior engineers. The job is to keep it that way while raising the ceiling: hand ICs ownership of entire domains, hold the bar on how work is scoped and delivered, and give feedback that changes outcomes. You have a real philosophy on how to hire, develop, and retain strong engineers, and clear written and verbal communication is how you exercise it.
