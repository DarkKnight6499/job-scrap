Position Summary The VPII of Enterprise AI Strategy within LPL’s Transformation Office exists to ensure that LPL’s AI strategy drives and shapes a multi-year roadmap that delivers AI-native products that transform our advisor and employee experience. This role is responsible for partnering deeply with our enterprise strategy, and enterprise AI product and technology leaders to ensure effectiveness of the firm’s AI strategy. In addition, the VPII runs point as Portfolio Lead for the enterprise AI portfolio— the connective tissue across business, product, technology, risk, and legal that turns strategic intent into a business outcome. This is a business-seat role requiring strategic fluency balanced with business outcome-focused execution capabilities and the ability to drive and align across the enterprise. What you'll own Pillar 1 —AI Strategy & Business Alignment Parter with enterprise strategy and AI product and tech to set the firm's business strategy for AI: where we're going, why, and in what order. This means partnering directly with the product and technology organization to translate strategic direction into a roadmap they can build against, and tying that strategy to the prioritization and funding decisions that determine what actually gets resourced. Strategy that isn't connected to a budget line is a wish list; this pillar exists to make sure that never happens. Outcomes that define success: A living AI strategy, revisited on a defined cadence, that product and technology leaders treat as their prioritization input rather than a document they nod at once a year Funding and resourcing decisions for AI initiatives traceable back to a stated strategic rationale, not made ad hoc Demonstrated ability to shift the roadmap in response to a new signal — a competitor move, a regulatory shift, a cost curve change — inside a single planning cycle, not a fiscal year Product and technology leaders describe the strategy function as a partner that removes ambiguity, not one that adds a layer of review Pillar 2 — Environmental Scanning & Competitive Intelligence Own the firm's outward-facing view of the AI landscape: what's changing in the industry, what competitors are doing, and what it means for us before it becomes obvious to everyone else. This pillar turns that scanning into decisions — gaining alignment across the firm on build-versus-buy calls, pricing and monetization approaches for AI-enabled offerings, and the operating model under which we deploy AI capability. Outcomes that define success: A standing view of the competitive and industry landscape that decision-makers actually use when a build-vs-buy or vendor question comes up Build-versus-buy positions documented, defensible, and revisited as the market shifts underneath them A pricing and monetization point of view for AI-enabled products that finance, product, and business leaders are aligned behind A clearly articulated AI deployment operating model — how capability gets built, governed, and rolled out — that the organization can point to instead of relitigating each time Pillar 3 — Enterprise AI Portfolio Leadership Act as Portfolio Lead for the enterprise AI program: the person accountable for making sure the firm actually executes on the strategy this role sets. That means working continuously with key leads across business, product, technology, risk, and legal — not as a status-collector, but as the person who surfaces where execution is at risk, resolves the cross-functional tradeoffs that block progress, and keeps the program moving toward business outcomes rather than activity. Outcomes that define success: The enterprise AI portfolio has a single, credible view of status, risk, and dependencies that business, product, technology, risk, and legal leaders all recognize as accurate Cross-functional blockers get resolved in days, not quarters, because this role owns getting the right people aligned fast Business outcomes tied to the enterprise AI portfolio — not just delivery milestones — are tracked and reported on a cadence leadership trusts Risk and legal are engaged as design partners early in the roadmap, not as a late-stage gate Cross-cutting: Alignment and organizational speed Underneath all three pillars is the thing this role is actually hired for: the ability to gain alignment across constituencies and to do it fast. Strategy, competitive intelligence, and portfolio execution all depend on the same muscle — building a case, bringing skeptical stakeholders along, and making a call when consensus won't fully form. This role is accountable for exercising that muscle daily, in the room, not just in the strategy document. This role carries outcomes on three scoreboards. Each matters; none alone is sufficient. For our product and technology partners. Strategic direction that arrives early enough and clearly enough to shape the roadmap, not react to it. Prioritization and funding calls that hold up under scrutiny because they were tied to strategy from the start. Fewer AI initiatives stalled or reworked because of ambiguity this role should have resolved upstream. For the firm's business outcomes. Build-versus-buy, pricing and monetization, and operating-model decisions that are made once, documented, and don't need to be relitigated every quarter. A competitive posture that reflects where the industry is actually heading, not where it was when the strategy was last updated. An enterprise AI portfolio that converts strategic intent into measurable business outcomes on a predictable cadence. For executive leadership and cross-functional partners. Business, product, technology, risk, and legal leaders who experience this role as the reason things move, not the reason they wait. A program status leadership can trust without a second read. Watch-items — competitive shifts, regulatory direction, vendor and platform risk, execution risk inside the program — surfaced early and reported on a cadence leadership relies on. What we're looking fo r: This is a business-seat role within the firm’s transformation office. We are weighting judgment, alignment, and speed of execution technical depth. Demonstrated experience owning AI strategy from a business seat, including translating strategic direction into funding and prioritization decisions that a product and technology organization actually executed against A track record of gaining alignment across business, product, technology, risk, and legal stakeholders who did not start out aligned — and doing it on a timeline the business needed, not the timeline that was comfortable Fluency in the commercial and competitive dimensions of AI: build-versus-buy tradeoffs, pricing and monetization models for AI-enabled products, and the operating models firms use to deploy AI at scale Experience running environmental scanning and competitive intelligence as a decision-making input, not a reporting exercise — you can point to calls that changed because of what you surfaced Direct experience as a program or initiative lead for cross-functional, multi-stakeholder efforts, comfortable being the accountable owner when no single function can drive outcomes alone A demonstrated balance of strategic agility and operational excellence — equally credible setting direction and getting into the operational detail required to make that direction real Industry chops: enough firsthand exposure to how AI is actually being built, bought, priced, and deployed across the industry to have an informed, current point of view — not a secondhand one The ability to move fast across many aspects of the work simultaneously, without needing the organization to slow down and wait for sequential handoffs BA/BS required; MBA or equivalent advanced degree a plus. You will be a fit for this role if the prospect of owning the connective tissue between AI strategy and AI execution — and being the person the firm counts on to gain alignment and make it move — is more interesting than owning one piece of the AI stack in isolation.
